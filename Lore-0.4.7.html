Added

  • Daily Notes is now a day-centered timeline on iPhone. A compact week strip keeps nearby dates within reach, the month title expands into a full calendar, and each day brings its daily note together with the other notes created that day. Missing daily notes can be created directly from the view.
  • Suggested Links find connections while you write. The context panel now offers conservative, on-device suggestions when prose resembles an existing note title — including small name variations such as ElizaJane → Eliza. Inspect the exact phrase, link it without changing its visible wording, or dismiss the suggestion. No AI, API key, or network connection is required.
  • Text size for notes. On the Mac, View ▸ Bigger, Smaller, and Actual Size (⌘=, ⌘−, ⌘0) or Settings ▸ Appearance scale the editor from 14 to 28pt. On iPhone and iPad, notes follow the system text size. Spacing, headings, code, checkboxes, and the column width all scale with it.

Changed

  • Nearby Notes are pickier, on Mac, iPad, and iPhone. Lore now reads whole notes side by side and suggests only the few you'd likely have linked yourself — at most three, and often none. Each card quotes the line from the other note that makes the connection, notes that push back on yours come first, and suggestions you ignore teach Lore what you don't want to see. Existing suggestions refresh as you open notes. Without an OpenAI key, Nearby Notes work as before.
  • Nearby Notes explain themselves more plainly. Each card is one short, casual sentence about why the note belongs beside yours, with a line or two of detail when you open it — no filler or academic wording.
  • Linking a Nearby Note can fit it into your sentence. When you click Link, Lore may suggest a light rewrite of one paragraph so the link reads naturally. It's always shown as a draft for you to accept or reject, and rejecting restores your paragraph exactly.
  • Unlinked mentions now share the quieter visual style of backlinks. Their surrounding passage remains visible, while the card background and leading icon have been removed and the link action is now a subtle trailing icon.
  • Calmer, more readable note typography. Paragraphs no longer show a double gap from the blank line between them, headings sit closer to the text they introduce, and the writing column is slightly narrower for a more comfortable line length. Smaller headings are bolder and easier to tell apart, code blocks are a touch smaller, table numbers line up, and opening quotation marks hang into the margin. Letter pairs like "To" and "AV" are kerned again.
  • One typeface across the app. Note cards, previews, and onboarding now use the same system font as the editor.
  • iPhone notes read more like a book. Body text is a little smaller with tighter line spacing, and long words hyphenate lightly to avoid ragged gaps.

Fixed

  • System alerts no longer clip at the largest iPhone text sizes. The Terms & Privacy notice and similar prompts now keep their buttons fully visible with accessibility text sizes on, while your notes still scale.
  • Tags at the end of a note stay committed after reopening. Restoring the caret after a trailing tag no longer makes the tag look like unfinished input; newly typed tags still keep their muted in-progress state until committed.
  • The Mac formatting bar no longer flashes between appearances. When Lore follows the system appearance, the selection toolbar now uses the editor window's light or dark appearance from its first frame and tracks later appearance changes without a visible fade.
